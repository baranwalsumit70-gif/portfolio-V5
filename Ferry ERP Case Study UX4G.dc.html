01 · Context

A river crossing, run on paper.

Ferries across the Brahmaputra link Guwahati and North Guwahati. Most of the system is still manual, which slows decisions, risks safety and frustrates passengers.

{{ u.n }}{{ u.t }}
02 · Problem

No one sees the whole river.

Without a central, real-time system, each terminal works alone. That leads to five recurring failures.

{{ f.n }}{{ f.t }}
03 · Research

From the ghats to the system.

Field visits to Mazgaon, Rajaduar and Madhyamkhanda ghats, then systems mapping, two personas and three role-based flows.

{{ st.n }}{{ st.t }}{{ st.d }}
04 · Insight

Most failures don't come from missing boats or terminals. They come from missing visibility.

05 · Design

Each module answers one question an authority asks every day. Pick one to see the screen.

07 · What I learned

Version 1 had no system.

I designed the first dashboard screen by screen, without a design system. It worked, but reviewing it later showed the cost.

{{ g.n }}{{ g.t }}{{ g.d }}

So I studied UX4G Design System 3.0, the Government of India's standard for public services, and rebuilt the ERP on it.
